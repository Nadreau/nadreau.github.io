A

Goldfish — universal AI memory

It watches your screen, remembers what you're doing, and tells your AI. Any AI.

I got into these tools early, and one thing kept bugging me: every session starts from scratch. I was pasting the same context into every chat — who I am, what I'm working on, where I left off. Memory felt like it should work like the clipboard: an OS feature, not something one app owns. So I spent some time building the fix.

Goldfish is a little goldfish that lives on your screen and remembers your stuff. It reads the screen into a local database that never leaves your machine, a browser extension feeds context into Claude and ChatGPT, and an MCP server gives agents save / recall / forget. People seem to like it. I filed the provisional patent that December and drafted it myself, no attorney — OS-level screen memory wasn't a crowded idea back then. It is now.

THE BET

Memory works like the clipboard. It can't belong to one AI app. It has to be an OS feature every AI can use.

DECISION

Local by default. It's watching your screen. That data doesn't get to leave your machine.

Venture build · Nade LLC

B

Portfolio intelligence for venture funds

One system that takes the labor out of running a fund. Built for any VC or PE portfolio.

I was deep in the startup world at Penn State, evaluating deals for the student-run funds, right as I was getting serious about AI — and the two collided when the funds brought me on to figure out their AI side. The idea I chased: take every labor-intensive task in running a portfolio off a human's plate. Every call and document lands in a brain for each company. Those brains build the fund-facing profiles and train the agents that keep them current. Market trackers watch each company's competitors, and an alert layer decides what actually needs a partner.

I ran the approach with Penn State's two student venture funds, where I evaluated 40+ startups across $2M+ in deal flow. Then I built four fund-specific versions, seeded with each fund's real portfolio, and pitched them as working software. Funds don't want AI. They want their Monday morning back.

DECISION

Fund-facing, not analyst-facing. The product is the partner's Monday answer, not a stack of research docs.

DECISION

Pitch with working software. Every demo was a live build seeded with that fund's actual portfolio.

Internal builds · Bluon

C

Inside Bluon

Two examples from a longer list: the marketing and sales motions, rebuilt as agentic systems.

The Bluon marketing machine

Bluon doesn't have a marketing team. It has a brain, a fleet of agents, and me. The Brain is a knowledge base holding our campaign pillars, our voice, and the commercial facts generic AI gets wrong; every agent reads it before it acts. Ad results flow in nightly, sales calls flow in as transcripts, and finished work ships out the other side: creatives sized for every placement, emails in our voice that execs approve before they send, reports people actually read. My favorite part is the feedback loop. When an exec corrects wording, the correction becomes a default in the Brain, and every agent inherits it permanently. Twenty-six agents run pieces of this on schedules across ten platforms, and a health dashboard tells on the ones that break.

The sales intelligence loop

Every sales call gets used three times. It's graded: an agent scores each pitch and kickoff against our rubric and sends every rep a coaching digest that evening. It enriches the CRM: the moments that matter get attached to the deal. And it comes back around: before the next call, the rep gets a brief built from everything we know about the account. When my first grader scored harsher than the house standard, reps tuned it out. The rubric re-anchors on their feedback now.
