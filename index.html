Shipped product · patent pending since 12/2025

A

Goldfish — universal AI memory

It watches your screen, remembers what you're doing, and tells your AI. Any AI.

I got into these tools early, and one thing kept bugging me. Every session starts from scratch. I was pasting the same context into every chat — who I am, what I'm working on, where I left off. It felt like memory should work the way the clipboard does, something the operating system gives you that everything can use. So I spent some time building the fix.

Goldfish is a little goldfish that lives on your screen and remembers your stuff. It reads the screen into a local database that never leaves your machine, a browser extension feeds context into Claude and ChatGPT, and an MCP server gives agents save / recall / forget. People seem to like it. I filed the provisional patent that December and drafted it myself, no attorney — OS-level screen memory wasn't a crowded idea back then. It is now.

MY BET

Memory will eventually work the way the clipboard does now — an OS feature every app can use, not something one AI owns.

MY BET

The always-growing personal memory doesn't really exist anywhere yet. I think it gets built at the operating system, local first — and portability comes once it exists.

Venture build · Nade LLC

B

Portfolio intelligence for venture funds

One system that takes the labor out of running a fund. Built for any VC or PE portfolio.

I was deep in the startup world at Penn State, evaluating deals for the student-run funds, right as I was getting serious about AI — and the two collided when the funds brought me on to figure out their AI side. The idea I chased: take every labor-intensive task in running a portfolio off a human's plate. Every call and document lands in a brain for each company. Those brains build the fund-facing profiles and train the agents that keep them current. Market trackers watch each company's competitors, and an alert layer decides what actually needs a partner.

I ran the approach with Penn State's two student venture funds, where I evaluated 40+ startups across $2M+ in deal flow. Then I built four fund-specific versions, seeded with each fund's real portfolio, and pitched them as working software. Funds don't want AI. They want their Monday morning back.

MY BET

Most of the diligence grind is reading, and reading is what AI just got great at. A fund shouldn't need an analyst army to stay on top of its own portfolio.

HOW I PITCHED

With working software. Every demo was a live build seeded with that fund's actual portfolio.

Inside Bluon

Two examples from a longer list — one where AI runs the whole motion, one where it sharpens the people in it.

The Bluon marketing machine

As AI gets more capable and more integrated, it gets easier to frame a clear end goal and build a system around getting it done. Marketing was the perfect pillar to start with. At our scale, most of the day-to-day work — ad creation, ad management, email generation, reporting — is work I think a well-built system does better than a person. So I built the machine that covers the whole thing. At the center is the Brain, a knowledge base holding our campaign pillars, our voice, and the commercial facts generic AI gets wrong, and every agent reads it before acting. My favorite part is the feedback loop. When an exec corrects wording, the correction becomes a default in the Brain and every agent inherits it permanently. Twenty-six agents run pieces of this on schedules across ten platforms, and a health dashboard tells on the ones that break.

The sales intelligence loop

Sales is the opposite case, and the comparison is the interesting part. You want humans in that loop — the job is relationships. So instead of replacing the process, the system plugs in beside it and sharpens the people running it. Every call gets graded against our rubric, with a coaching digest to each rep that evening. The CRM gets enriched with the moments that matter. Before the next call, the rep gets a brief built from everything we know about the account. When my first grader scored harsher than the house standard, reps tuned it out, so the rubric re-anchors on their feedback now. Marketing shows what AI can run outright. Sales shows how it amplifies the people who stay in the loop.
