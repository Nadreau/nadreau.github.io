Goldfish — universal AI memory

Copy and paste for your memory. Whatever you're working on follows you into any AI.

I got into these tools early, and one thing kept annoying me. On every project, I was pasting a pile of context into the chat just to get a simple answer out of the AI. It felt like memory should work the way the clipboard does, an easy handoff that follows you everywhere, handled by the operating system instead of any one app. So I spent some time building the fix.

Goldfish is a little goldfish that lives on your screen and remembers your stuff. It reads the screen into a local database that never leaves your machine, a browser extension feeds context into Claude and ChatGPT, and an MCP server gives agents save / recall / forget. People seem to like it. I filed the provisional patent that December and drafted it myself, no attorney. OS-level screen memory wasn't a crowded idea back then. It is now.

MY BET

Memory will eventually work the way the clipboard does now. An OS feature every app can use, not something one AI owns.

MY BET

The always-growing personal memory doesn't exist anywhere yet. I think it shows up at the operating system layer first, and locally.

B

Portfolio intelligence for venture funds

One system that takes the labor out of running a fund. Built for any VC or PE portfolio.

I got deep into consulting in school, and I fell in love with startups and venture capital, the whole idea of being part of a company's growth. Right as that was happening, AI was getting seriously good, and I was early in seeing what it could do for a fund's actual goals. So I built an agentic flow around them. It takes over the heavy lifts like due diligence, and it keeps up with the things that are genuinely hard to keep up with, like the markets and industries around every startup in the portfolio.

I ran the approach with Penn State's two student venture funds, where I evaluated 40+ startups across $2M+ in deal flow. Then I built four fund-specific versions, each seeded with that fund's real portfolio, and pitched them as working software. Funds don't want AI. They want their Monday morning back.

MY BET

Most of the diligence grind is reading, and reading is what AI just got great at. A fund shouldn't need an analyst army to stay on top of its own portfolio.

HOW I PITCHED

With working software. Every demo was a live build seeded with that fund's actual portfolio.

Inside Bluon

Two examples from a longer list. One machine runs marketing. The other makes our sales team better.

The Bluon marketing machine

As AI gets more capable and more integrated, it gets easier to frame a clear end goal and build a system around getting it done. Marketing was the perfect pillar to start with. At our scale, most of the day-to-day work, from ad creation to emails to reporting, is work I think a well-built system does better than a person. So I built the machine that covers the whole thing. Every agent reads the Brain before acting, a knowledge base with our campaign pillars, our voice, and the commercial facts generic AI gets wrong. My favorite part is the feedback loop. When an exec corrects wording, that correction becomes a default in the Brain and every agent inherits it. Twenty-six agents run this on schedules, and a dashboard tells on the ones that break.

The sales intelligence loop

Sales is the opposite case. You want humans in that loop, because the job is relationships. So the system plugs in beside the process and sharpens the people running it. Every call gets graded against our rubric, each rep gets a coaching digest that evening, the CRM picks up the moments that matter, and the next call starts with a brief built from everything we know about the account. Marketing shows what AI can run outright. Sales shows how it amplifies the people who stay in the loop.
